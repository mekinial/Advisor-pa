# Retirement: the "My Retirement Number" method

From the Planners Inc workshop deck *My Retirement Number* (2026) and the *Retirement Income Roadmap*
deck. Use the same four questions and wording style. Clients have heard them.

## The four questions

1. **What do I want?** Monthly retirement spending in today's dollars:
   **today's spending − what stops + what starts or grows.**
   - Stops: mortgage, CPF contributions, commuting and work costs, children's education, premiums that mature.
   - Starts or grows: healthcare and premiums, travel, hobbies, household help, contingency, long-term care.
   - The most common mistake is assuming retirement is cheaper. Check that the number funds the life they picture.
   - Lifestyle bands for a sense-check: Essential / Comfortable / Aspirational.
2. **What do I have?** CPF, SRS, investments, cash, property, insurance and income plans, business.
   Give each resource a job:
   - CPF: the foundation, guaranteed income for life
   - Insurance: protects the plan
   - Investments: growth first, income later
   - Cash: liquidity
   - Property: housing, possible income, legacy
3. **Where am I heading?** Convert each resource to monthly income:
   - Already monthly (CPF LIFE, annuity, rental, policy income): use it as it is.
   - A pot to draw down (SRS, cash, maturity proceeds): total ÷ years drawn ÷ 12. SRS is usually drawn over 10 years.
   - A pot to live off: **4% a year ÷ 12**. This is a rough planning convention, not a return anyone can
     promise. Always say "first sketch" or "illustrative".
   - Gap = want − projected.
4. **What can I do about it?** Six levers:
   - Time
   - Contributions (CPF top-ups, SRS, regular savings)
   - Growth
   - Income generation (annuities, CPF LIFE deferral, rental)
   - Protection
   - Expectations
   
   **Review before you buy:** look at idle cash, overlapping cover, unused CPF levers, a portfolio set up for
   the wrong phase, and property doing only one job.

## Life stages (pick the one that fits)

| Age | Persona | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 30–35 | The Builder | Foundation: starting early, protecting income |
| 40–45 | The Accumulator | Growth and protection: has lifestyle outgrown the planning? |
| 50–55 | The Optimiser | Closing gaps: am I actually on track? |
| 60+ | The Distributor | Income and legacy: sustainable income, living to 95, liquidity |

## Reference figures (2026, as published; check that they are current before quoting)

| CPF retirement sum (set aside at 55, 2026 cohort) | Amount | Est. CPF LIFE payout from 65 (Standard, male) |
|---|---|---|
| Basic (BRS) | $110,200 | ~$950/month |
| Full (FRS) | $220,400 | ~$1,780/month |
| Enhanced (ERS) | $440,800 | ~$3,440/month |

- Statutory retirement age is 64 from 1 July 2026 and re-employment age is 69. **CPF LIFE payouts still start at 65.**
  The two are not linked.
- The client's own CPF LIFE figure comes from the CPF Retirement Payout Planner or the CPF LIFE Estimator. Never use the
  table above as their number. Use it only to explain.
- The CareShield Life base payout is about $689/month in 2026, rising 4% a year to 2030. Care costs before subsidies:
  - home nursing: about $250–750/month
  - day care: about $900–1,400/month
  - VWO nursing home: about $2,500–4,500/month
  - private nursing home: about $3,500–6,000+/month
  
  Subsidies are means-tested.
- Inflation of 2.5% is the workshop convention. $5,000 today is about $6,400 in 10 years, $8,200 in 20 and $10,500 in 30.
- CPF cash top-ups to SA/RA give up to $8,000 tax relief, plus up to $8,000 more for top-ups to family members.

## Distribution phase (60+): the three-bucket approach (Retirement Income Roadmap)

1. **Liquidity** (now to 2 years): about 18 months of expenses plus a health buffer, in money-market funds, SSB or T-bills.
2. **Stable income**: an SSB ladder, an optional annuity and short-duration bonds, on top of CPF LIFE.
3. **Growth and income** (5+ years): market-linked funds for yield and as an inflation hedge.

Name the risks the plan must answer: longevity, inflation, sequence risk, liquidity and concentration.
Income funds can pay out of capital, and distributions are not guaranteed. Always say so.
